# Action, Performance, Dialogue, and Sound

## Action vs performance

Action = what happens.  
Performance = how this person physically carries it.

Useful internal chain:
`Trigger → Perception → Immediate Reaction → Decision → Action → Interaction → Feedback → Result`

Do not force every final shot to spell every node; expose only useful steps.

## Trigger before reaction

Never let a character react before the stimulus exists.

The character can only react to what they can actually perceive:
sight, sound, touch, pain, temperature, smell, vibration, or an established supernatural sense.

## Character-specific reaction

Different people react differently based on training, temperament, body state and social mask.

Do not clone wide eyes/open mouth/step-back across everyone.

## Natural performance

Emotion is not a fixed gesture map.

Avoid:
- fear = always step back
- anger = always clench fist
- grief = always cry
- shock = always stare

Important emotions may be hidden.

Use 1–3 meaningful performance signals per shot, not every brow/finger/breath muscle.

## Blinking/breathing

No mechanical blink frequency.

Use blink/breath only when story-visible, emotionally meaningful or physically relevant.

## Weight/body mechanics

For hard actions, respect weight shift, support, force direction, landing and final pose.

Do not write biomechanics for ordinary movement.

## Falls/impacts/injury

A fall has cause, imbalance, direction, landing and final pose.

A hit has force source, contact area, displacement and result.

Injury affects later movement until treated/time/world rule allows recovery.

## Hands/props

Lock left/right only when continuity needs it:
weapons, key prop close-up, grabbing, continuing same action, different hand tasks.

Do not let unique props teleport between hands without transfer.

## Paths

Important movement:
`start → path → destination`

Respect obstacles.

## Multi-character action

Establish:
- PRIMARY ACTION
- SECONDARY REACTION
- BACKGROUND ACTION

Specify who moves/speaks/attacks first.

Avoid all characters launching equally strong actions at once.

## Physical resource conflicts

A body cannot plausibly use the same hand for incompatible simultaneous actions. Check hand/body-task conflicts.

## Contact interaction

For pushes/grabs/strikes, state practical contact and result when contact matters.

AI fight design should favor fewer readable actions over long complex combinations.

## Emotional continuity

Track emotion as progression driven by triggers, not as asset state.

Public mask may differ from internal emotion.

Relationship can show through distance, address forms, who yields, who touches/blocks, who answers, who avoids gaze.

## Dialogue

Dialogue must match character, relationship, current knowledge, pressure and speech intention.

Useful `SPEECH_INTENT`:
question, probe, threaten, comfort, conceal, delay, provoke, refuse, request help, order, deflect.

Locked dialogue is verbatim.

Do not modify words to fit generation. Adjust timing/shots/grouping.

## Delivery

Final format may be:
`人物（语气＋语速＋必要语调）：“原台词。”`

Use only meaningful delivery notes.

Avoid adjective piles.

Speech rate follows character/state; no rigid characters-per-second output.

## Listener reaction

Important lines may be carried by listener reaction.

Do not cut to reaction after every sentence.

Require listener reaction when it changes relationship, information, emotion or next action.

## Overlap/interruption

If dialogue overlaps, state who starts and where interruption occurs.

Prefer concrete:
A: “你根本不知道——”
B cuts in: “我知道。”

over “they argue simultaneously”.

## Silence

No response can be an action.

Do not add dialogue merely to avoid silence.

## Lip sync

If precise speech matters, use shot scales/orientation where mouth is visible.

If distant/back-facing/occluded, do not rely on perfect sync.

In groups, identify speaker explicitly; if needed, state a critical non-speaker remains silent/listens.

## Dialogue duration

Natural speech must fit shot/group time.

If not:
- lengthen within capacity
- redistribute shots
- use continuous audio over reactions
- split group at semantic boundary

Never secretly accelerate locked dialogue unnaturally.

## Audio categories

DIALOGUE, VOICE_OVER, ENVIRONMENT, ACTION_SOUND, PROP_SOUND, CREATURE_SOUND, VFX_SOUND, SILENCE, MUSIC.

Music exists only when project allows it.

## Sound as event

Important sound may be Trigger.

Specify source/direction/distance when it drives blocking:
behind, door exterior, upstairs, distant, underground, phone/radio.

Sound should change spatial quality after doors open/close or characters move.

## Environment sound

Use 1–3 valuable bed sounds, not exhaustive inventories.

Track sustained sounds so alarms/fire/wind do not randomly restart or vanish.

## VFX/creature sound

Match form, scale, material and world rules.

Black smoke does not automatically need sci-fi energy hum. A supernatural event may deliberately be silent.

## BGM

`AUDIO_LOCK` dominates.

If `NO_BGM`, state once at group level; do not repeat every shot.

Music never substitutes for performance or causality.

## Final action/performance field

Seedance-facing description prioritizes:
trigger → visible reaction → core action/path → contact if needed → feedback → result.

Avoid literary psychology and repeated “natural/cinematic acting” labels.

## Scale with shot

Do not write eyelid micro-tremor for a wide shot. Do not demand five emotional changes in a 2s close-up.

Performance detail must be visible at current shot scale and feasible in current duration.

## AI standing test

If characters merely stand and speak, decide whether static tension is intentional.

If not, add only story-real behavior, not arbitrary pacing/touching/drinking.

## Animal/nonhuman acting

Real animals: ear/tail/body weight/sniffing/breathing, not human expressions.

Monsters: behavior follows body structure, weight, senses and rules.

Human-looking ghosts remain natural performers; abnormality can come from breath absence, timing, environmental interaction or rule-based VFX rather than zombie acting.
