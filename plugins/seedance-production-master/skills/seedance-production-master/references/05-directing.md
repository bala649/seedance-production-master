# Director Design, Shot Plan, Camera, Composition, and Editing

## Director objective

Directing decides when, where and how the audience receives approved story information.

Start with viewpoint, story task, spatial readability, relationship and reveal—not a favorite lens.

## Three levels

SCENE_DIRECTOR_DESIGN  
GROUP_DIRECTOR_DESIGN  
SHOT_PLAN

A group intent is one precise sentence about what the audience must understand/feel, not “make it cinematic”.

## Shot Plan

Before final prompt decide for each shot:
- story task
- viewpoint
- subject
- start state
- trigger
- camera side
- relative position
- height/angle
- shot scale
- focal logic
- composition/depth
- eyeline
- screen direction
- movement
- action/performance
- sound/VFX
- cut reason
- end state

Do not expose internal IDs or calculations.

## Shot necessity

A shot must serve story information, space, action, reaction, relationship/power, prop information, VFX/result, reveal/transition or necessary rhythm.

Do not mechanically shoot master/OTS/close-up coverage.

## Viewpoint

Possible:
objective, character-aligned, POV, OTS, object/creature POV, surveillance, reflection, occluded/door-gap.

POV must identify whose view and cannot simultaneously show that character's full body without a plausible reflection/screen mechanism.

## Axis and direction

Establish ACTION_AXIS for dialogue/action.

180° is not an absolute ban. Cross only through visible crossing, neutral reset, character movement/re-axis, clear new spatial establishment, or intentional disorientation.

Track screen direction in pursuit/travel. Sudden unexplained reversal can read as running back.

## Eyeline

Eyelines follow real world positions. Screen left/right may change on reverses while world relationship stays correct.

## Camera position

Final prompts may state useful geometry:
counter southwest side, right rear shoulder, inside doorway, coffin-head side.

Avoid arbitrary 2.14m / 43.7° precision unless uniquely necessary.

## Height/angle

Use floor/knee/waist/chest/eye/shoulder/high/top as needed.

Do not symbolically force villains low-angle and weak characters high-angle.

## Shot scale

Use scale that makes the important fact readable:
extreme wide, wide/full, medium full, medium, medium close, close, detail/extreme close.

Shot-scale progression follows information/emotional distance, not random jumps.

## Focal length

Lens serves space, perspective, face distortion, compression, depth and movement.

Use numeric focal lengths when helpful; natural descriptions are acceptable.

Do not default every emotional shot to 85mm.

## Composition

Consider foreground/action plane/background, looking room, lead room, negative space, symmetry/asymmetry, occlusion.

Composition's first job is readability.

Foreground obstruction should not hide critical action.

## Camera movement

Every movement answers “why now?”

Motives:
follow subject, reveal information, change relationship, increase pressure, reveal larger danger, shift attention, subjective exploration.

Fixed camera is a deliberate choice.

### Movement types

Push: discovery/focus/pressure.  
Pull: reveal situation/scale/isolation.  
Lateral: follow/reveal/spatial relationship.  
Orbit: high risk; use only when simple and valuable.  
Handheld: controlled immediacy, not random shake.  
Stabilized follow: good for traversal but drift risk rises with complexity.  
Pan/tilt: state start/end target.  
Zoom: distinguish from dolly.  
Rack focus: state focus A → B.

Prefer one dominant camera action per shot.

## Cut logic

Cuts need triggers:
line landing, head turn, door opening, object fall, sound, contact, reveal, reaction.

Do not insert a reaction after every line.

## Dialogue directing

Establish enough spatial relationship before tight coverage.

OTS must preserve foreground identity/shoulder side/eyeline.

When characters stand/cross/circle/block doors, update axis.

Long dialogue may remain sustained if performance/relationship carries it.

## Action directing

Priority:
`who does what > where > result > camera flourish`

Pursuits need readable pursuer/target/distance/goal/obstacle/direction.

Impacts need visible results.

Use insert shots only when detail must be understood.

## VFX directing

At critical stages, make source/path/target/result understandable.

Do not let smoke/particles constantly obscure performance.

Large effects need at least one readable scale relationship.

## Horror/mystery

Prefer limited view, offscreen space, deep composition, door gaps, reflection, static anomaly and foreground obstruction.

Do not default to fish-eye, extreme Dutch, flashing or random shake.

## Light/depth

Define source logic and story emphasis:
where light comes from, what is visible/hidden, whether VFX changes light.

Do not invent impossible light or swap key direction without cause.

Depth of field serves information.

## Long takes

Use when continuous space/performance/ritual matters.

Risk rises with many characters, position changes, prop exchanges, VFX and large traversal.

Director value must justify generation risk.

## First/last shot

First shot establishes current state or immediate trigger.

Last shot completes the group event and yields a stable end state. Stable does not mean frozen.

## Physical continuity

Every shot end is compatible with next shot start:
position, pose, prop, injury, environment, VFX.

## Seedance-facing camera language

Prefer short natural execution language:
`35mm中景；摄影机在柜台西南侧、人物眼平高度，固定。`

Do not explain why the lens was chosen.

## Director previz

Run the group from 00:00 to end and inspect blocking, camera side, cuts, eyelines, screen direction, props, VFX, performance, sound and end state.

Repair order:
space → action → axis → composition → movement → shot scale → beauty.
